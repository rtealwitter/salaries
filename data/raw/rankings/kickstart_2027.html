U.S. News & World Report released their 2027 college rankings recently.  Nearly 1,500 schools were ranked based on 17 factors, with a few key changes to the methodology compared to the previous year.  

New Carnegie classifications reshuffle the categories

The biggest change to the methodology is that rankings are now built on the 2025 Carnegie Classification instead of the 2021 version. This remaps schools’ categories (National Universities, National Liberal Arts Colleges, Regional Universities, or Regional Colleges). With roughly one in five schools changing category this year, a college can change rank simply because its grouping changed.

A new earnings measure replaces student debt

U.S. News dropped median federal loan debt as a factor and replaced it with a new Earnings by Major indicator, still weighted at 5%. The new measure looks at what graduates earn four years out, using College Scorecard data limited to students who received federal financial aid. U.S. News argues that this switch gives a cleaner read on what the school itself adds in salary potential, rather than a reflection of family resources or of enrollment weighted toward high-paying fields.

Rankings shifts

MIT claims the top spot for National Universities, unseating Princeton, while Williams College retains its position atop the Liberal Arts Colleges list. Check the tables below for full details.
